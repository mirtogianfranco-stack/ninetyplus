Nobody saw this coming.
Brighton are second in the Premier League. They beat Arsenal, they won at the Etihad, and they have the best goal difference in the country.
So, are Brighton for real? We went through the numbers. Four parts. The hype, the case for, the catch, and the verdict.
Part one. The hype.
After ten games, Arsenal lead on twenty seven points. Brighton are second, on twenty two, one point ahead of Manchester City.
They have scored twenty six goals, more than anyone in the league.
They have conceded just seven. Their goal difference, plus nineteen, is the best in the Premier League by nine.
And they have kept seven clean sheets, level with Arsenal for the most in the league.
Part two. The case for.
Arsenal have lost one league game all season. It was at Brighton, three nil.
Then Brighton went to the Etihad and beat Manchester City, one nil. And on Sunday, they beat ten man Brentford, two nil.
This is not a team that only beats the bottom half. On average, they take more shots than their opponents. Sixteen point nine a game, against eleven point four.
And they put five point two shots on target per game, fourth best in the league.
Part three. The catch.
Across the league, about one in three shots on target ends up in the net. Thirty two percent.
Brighton score with half of theirs. Fifty percent. The highest rate in the league.
At the other end, opponents score with just twenty one percent of their shots on target against Brighton.
If Brighton finished and defended like an average team, their goal difference would be about plus six. Not plus nineteen.
And fourteen of their twenty six goals came in just three games. Four nil against Villa, five nil at Coventry, and five nil against Palace.
Eleven different players have scored, and nobody has more than four.
Part four. The verdict.
Brighton are good. They have beaten Arsenal and City, and they control games.
But the finishing is running hot, and finishing like that usually cools off.
Our verdict? A top four team, for real. Title challengers? Not yet.
