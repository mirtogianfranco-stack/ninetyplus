Something broke at Manchester City.
They won their first seven league games. Then they lost three in a row, to Villa, Brighton and Forest.
So, what is wrong with City? We went through the numbers. Four parts. The collapse, the surprise, the real problem, and the verdict.
Part one. The collapse.
In the first seven games, City scored nineteen goals and conceded seven.
In the last three, they scored two, and conceded six.
They have dropped from top of the table to third, six points behind Arsenal.
Part two. The surprise.
Here is the thing. City did not get worse at creating chances.
In the winning run, they took fourteen point six shots a game. In the losing run, sixteen.
And they gave up fewer shots, not more. Eleven a game in the winning run. Under nine in the losing run.
Part three. The real problem.
It is finishing, at both ends.
In the winning run, half of City's shots on target went in. In the last three games, just twelve percent.
The league average is thirty two.
At the other end, opponents scored with a quarter of their shots on target in the winning run. In the losing run, sixty percent.
And Erling Haaland had eight goals in the first seven games. One in the last three.
Part four. The verdict.
City are still creating far more than they allow. That is not a broken team. That is a bad run of finishing.
Our verdict? City are back in the title race by Christmas.
